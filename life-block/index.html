The rules
  • Conway's rule: a cell is born with exactly 3 live neighbours and survives with 2 or 3.
  • A newborn cell takes the block that owns at least 2 of its 3 parents; three different parents give a neutral cell.
  • One generation is one Base block (0.2 s). The brain of each block beats every 10 blocks.
  • Excited (buys, new holders): it launches gliders, one per 3 buys (up to 3, fanned out); on a burst of 5 buys or more, it blooms.
  • The market sets the speed: when buys run at least twice the sells (3 buys or more), the block rushes a fast ship, twice a glider's speed.
  • Each new holder, whatever the mood, settles an outpost (a beehive) beyond the edge: the colony spreads where its holders arrive.
  • Worried (more sells than buys): it sets a still shield toward its nearest rival; on a burst of 5 sells, a wall.
  • Curious (its own neurons busy, no news): it sends a scout ship. Calm and small: it keeps a heartbeat.
  • Predation: a living cell with 2 neighbours of one rival block, and fewer of its own, is eaten: it takes that rival's colour. No cell appears or dies, so Conway's rule stays exact; only the owner changes.
  • Combinations: excited with a rival in reach, it hunts (a glider aimed at the rival); curious with a rival in reach, it lays an eater in its path; worried and losing a fifth of its cells, it sends a spore away from danger; any block whose cells were eaten strikes back at the block that ate the most.
  • The crown is the goal: an excited block that does not wear it aims one more glider at the leader when the leader is within 80 cells. Everybody plays for the crown, so the leader is everybody's target.
  • Machines: on a burst of 8 buys in one beat, the brain builds a glider gun well outside its edge. Once built, the gun fires a glider every 30 generations on its own, until something breaks it.
  • Convert: on a beat with 3 buys or more, a block that touches a rival turns a 3x3 patch of the rival's cells into its own. It is the only gesture that writes over a rival, and it is what breaks frozen borders.
  • Revive: anyone can pay $1 of TBLOCK (to the holders' vault) to give a block in the game a fresh colony near its own and refill all its gestures.
  • Seasons: a season lasts 24 h of chain. When it ends, every block of it starts the next one on a new map drawn from the new season's seed: the game never stops.
  • Rain: every hour of chain, neutral seeds fall near the centre. Any colony can absorb them.
  • Wear: every 2 minutes of chain, one in six of the cells that did not change since the last check (same place, same block) dies. A colony that stops moving melts.
  • Alliances: two blocks that share a holder (an address that received exactly these two blocks, not a router that receives many) do not eat or convert each other.
  • Boss: the largest colony of the season before starts the next one with a glider gun already built. Everybody hunts it.
  • Catch-up: at mid-season, the five smallest colonies get a free revive.
  • Asleep: nothing. A block whose market goes quiet stops answering, and Life erodes it.
  • A gesture only writes empty cells. Budgets per hour cap every gesture; past 24,000 live cells, growth gestures wait.
  • The crown: each beat, the block with the most live cells wears it (a tie leaves it where it was). The feed on the side tells who takes it, who eats whom, who is wiped out and who comes back.
  • Start places come from each address's keccak hash, on a spiral: nobody picks a spot.
  • The brain reacts by fixed rules from its mood. It does not plan.

Crown market

Who wears the crown at the end of this round? Bets in TBLOCK, open in the first half of each 10-minute round.

Winners share the pot, 5 % goes to the holders' vault. A result can be challenged for 6 min (bond: the larger of $5 or 10 % of the pot): a challenged round is refunded. No result in time: refunded. Bets can be lost.
